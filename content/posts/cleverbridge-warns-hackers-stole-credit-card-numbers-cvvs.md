Cleverbridge detected the breach on 4th November 2026 and went offline temporarily. They state that all systems were back online on that day, though their subsequent investigation revealed that a third party had copied the payment card data that was stored on their platform.

This is via [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/).

Cleverbridge provide e-commerce services to software and SaaS companies. They haven't specified which of their customers’ businesses were impacted. They started notifying their affected customers on 20th November.

Cleverbridge’s public disclosure didn't state which card details were copied. A notification issued to [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/) stated that the information copied included the card number, the expiry date and the CVV/CVC security code used in the purchase via the platform. It also confirmed that no other personal information (such as names, contact details or billing information) was copied. The card number was shown as ending in XXXX, with [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/) having redacted the last four digits.

The company has not said how the attackers got in, how long they were in the system or how many customers were impacted. Cleverbridge has confirmed it was not a ransomware attack and that their systems were not encrypted.

Cleverbridge has informed the data protection authorities, law enforcement, the payment card networks and the payment partners. It is working with TransUnion’s Cyberscout to issue the notifications and to provide affected customers with complimentary credit monitoring and fraud assistance.

The company is advising affected customers to check their card statements for any unauthorised transactions and to get in touch with their card issuers. Banks may choose to replace the cards in question.

[BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/) asked Cleverbridge how many customers were impacted, how the attackers had got in, whether the card data that had been stolen was encrypted and why the CVV/CVC numbers were available. Cleverbridge had not got back to them.
