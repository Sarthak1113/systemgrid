S&P Global Ratings is currently looking at Nvidia’s credit rating with the potential for downgrades arising from serious issues around corporate governance, executive oversight and the degree to which the company is reliant on CEO Jensen Huang.

Despite the sheer financial success of Nvidia, the recent incidents surrounding Huang's reported sacking and then his reinstatement have raised all sorts of concerns about the state of the leadership structure and the company's succession planning. S&P have pointed to a number of issues with executive oversight and have specifically highlighted what they term as "key man risk' in the context of the company's continued ability to innovate and grow.

The credit rating agency will complete its review in 90 days. If a downgrade is warranted then Nvidia's rating could be downgraded from AA to AA- but it would still be investment grade.

A downgrade could well lead to higher borrowing costs for Nvidia and have a significant impact on the financing of AI infrastructure. Nvidia is a key player in the financing of neocloud providers and other infrastructure companies who generally have weaker credit ratings. A downgrade could therefore lead to higher costs for those companies to borrow or could see lenders demanding more security.

This is of course absolutely critical as Nvidia moves forward with its big AI infrastructure financing play. In August they announced their intent to arrange up to $500bn in financing with a number of the big institutions (Goldman Sachs, Apollo, Blackstone etc). Originally they were expecting to be the guarantor on 25% of the loans but as Bloomberg reports the lenders have been pushing for a much larger guarantee from Nvidia, highlighting the continued uncertainty around Huang's position.

If S&P do decide to downrate Nvidia then the lenders could end up demanding even more security from the company and that would massively increase its exposure to the growing debt in the AI infrastructure space.

This is a review which really does highlight the importance of governance and leadership in determining credit ratings. It's a case in point of how even the most profitable companies at the very heart of the global AI movement have to have governance and leadership in place if they are to maintain investor confidence.
