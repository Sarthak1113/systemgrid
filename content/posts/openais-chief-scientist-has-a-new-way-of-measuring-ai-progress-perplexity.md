In the midst of the discussion about whether frontier AI development should be throttled, OpenAI’s head scientist has suggested we adopt some common metrics to track the rate of progress. One of these is ‘perplexity' (which has nothing to do with the AI search company of that name).

In a very detailed blog post on Sunday 22nd November, Jakub Pachocki defines perplexity as 'how well a model is able to predict the next token on average'. Lower perplexity is better.

Perplexity and the like would be common ceilings "that developers are committed to not breach until our ability to understand and monitor AI behaviour has reached a certain concrete milestone", he wrote.

Pachocki’s views were fully supported by OpenAI CEO Sam Altman who in a post on X called the essay "the most important thing OpenAI has published in awhile". He also stated that such limits would be a way of ensuring safety and control.

The proposal comes at a time when there is increasing concern both within and outside the industry about the potential risks of AI’s rapidly escalating capabilities.

Former OpenAI and Anthropic researchers in September said they had left their jobs because AI systems could become capable of destroying humanity fairly soon. The newly filed Anthropic IPO also indicated that the technology could present “catastrophic or existential risks to society".

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei then called on the major AI companies and research labs to “pace the frontier' with a plan that would involve the companies agreeing to common safety standards as well as limits on the rate of unimpeded AI progress amongst other things. The plan was supported by Altman and SpaceX CEO Elon Musk, though others in the tech industry were less than happy with it with former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and White House advisor David Sacks being amongst those who disagreed.

Pachocki’s Sunday piece is all about measuring the rate of AI progress. That could well be the first step that we need to take before we start to put speed limits on it.
